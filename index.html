Peng Luo
Assistant Professor
School of Earth, Environment, and Sustainability, University of Iowa

I am a tenure-track Assistant Professor in the School of Earth, Environment, and Sustainability at the University of Iowa, where I lead research at the intersection of GIScience, GeoAI, and spatial data science. Previously, I was a postdoctoral researcher at the MIT Senseable City Lab, and I received my Ph.D. from the Chair of Cartography and Visual Analytics at the Technical University of Munich. My research focuses on Trustworthy GeoAI, with particular interests in spatial explicit modeling, geospatial explainable AI, uncertainty quantification for GeoAI models, and multi-agent GeoAI.

Research Interests

GeoAI is increasingly used to support decisions about cities, health, and the environment, but no GeoAI model is perfect: its predictions are uncertain, and that uncertainty varies across space. I develop trustworthy GeoAI methods that make geospatial models more transparent, reliable, and reproducible, around three connected themes:

  • Spatially explicit modeling: Models that account for spatial heterogeneity and geographic context in prediction and association analysis, such as generalized heterogeneity models for interpolation, focal-feature regression kriging, and geographically optimal zones-based heterogeneity models.
  • Trustworthy GeoAI: Explainable AI and uncertainty quantification that reveal not only what a model predicts, but how reliable each prediction is and where, including GeoConformal prediction, uncertainty of spatial explanations (GeoXCP), uncertainty for nonstationary spatial processes, and visual analytics of predictability.
  • Transferable and scalable GeoAI: Methods that generalize across regions, datasets, and tasks, and multi-agent large language models for geospatial model discovery, including GeoEvolve, self-evolving geospatial algorithms (Annals of the AAG, in press), and graph-based deep spatial regression.
