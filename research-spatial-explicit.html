Spatial Process Modeling

Spatial Heterogeneity · Interaction · Regimes · Prediction
Overview

Spatial data mixes pattern with noise. This research line develops models that recover the processes behind spatial patterns: how geographical variables interact, how relationships change across space, where one spatial regime gives way to another, and how far spatial structure makes a variable predictable. The aim is to ask simple questions and answer them with the simplest method that works.

Key methods include geographically optimal zones-based heterogeneity models (GOZH and Robust GOZH) and LESH for detecting interactions among geographical variables, GPI for measuring spatially varying effects within interacting patterns, RegimeBench for testing how well classical and XAI-driven approaches recover nonlinear spatial regimes, graph-based deep spatial regression for process heterogeneity, and GHM and MSAM for accurate spatial prediction from small samples. GIP models shared e-scooter mobility as a label propagation process to uncover overlapping spatial communities.

Key Contributions
  • RegimeBench: a systematic benchmark of classical and XAI-driven approaches for nonlinear spatial regimes (IJGIS 2026)
  • GPI: measuring univariate effects in the interaction of geographical patterns (IJGIS 2026)
  • Focal-feature regression kriging (Geographical Analysis 2026)
  • Graph-based deep spatial regression for spatial process heterogeneity (ISPRS JPRS 2026)
  • GHM: generalized heterogeneity model for spatial interpolation (IJGIS 2023, top-1 most read and top-2 most cited)
  • MSAM: multi-scale spatial association modeling with extremely sparse data (ISPRS JPRS 2025)
  • GOZH and Robust GOZH: geographically optimal zones-based heterogeneity models (ISPRS JPRS 2022; GIScience & Remote Sensing 2025)
  • GIP: overlapping communities in shared e-micromobility (CEUS 2025)
  • Book chapter (forthcoming): Spatial Stratified Modeling in International Encyclopedia of Geography (Wiley-AAG)
Software
